Energy storage · Optimization / INDEPENDENT PROJECT

Optimizing battery dispatch transparently.

How does an assumed cycling cost change arbitrage margin and battery cycling? A historical model study for the German power market.

01 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Implementation

  • Dispatch optimization using mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) with SciPy/HiGHS and physical consistency checks.
  • Compare cycling-cost scenarios using 2024 SMARD prices.

02 / METHODOLOGY

Data and approach

  1. 1 MW / 2 MWh battery energy storage system (BESS); state of charge (SOC) between 10 and 90%; each delivery day starts and ends at 50% SOC.
  2. 8,784 hours in leap year 2024, DE/LU day-ahead prices. Local delivery days contain 23, 24 or 25 hours to account for daylight saving.
  3. 95% charging and 95% discharging efficiency, equivalent to 90.25% round-trip efficiency. Binary variables prevent simultaneous charge and discharge.
  4. Daily optimization with known historical prices. Cycling costs act as an economic proxy; schedules are replayed through the battery model.

03 / RESULT

Results in context

32.44% fewer equivalent full cycles than optimization without assumed cycling costs.

Metrics from the documented analysis
Net arbitrage margin (simulated)44,685.44 €
Equivalent full cycles418.30 EFC
Fewer equivalent full cycles vs optimization without assumed cycling costs32.44 %
Lower gross arbitrage margin vs optimization without assumed cycling costs5.27 %

The comparison is between optimized scenarios at €20 and €0 per MWh of equivalent-cycle energy. The fixed reference schedule is a different baseline and contains 292.80 EFC.

SCOPE

Scope and limitations.

Retrospective modeling, not realized commercial earnings or a validated trading strategy. Capital, grid, tax and other project costs are excluded. Fewer modeled cycles do not establish measured lifetime extension; no electrochemical aging model.
